Maximum-likelihood Bradley-Terry ratings over the whole round robin -- 6 agents, 15 pairings, 500 games each, every one on a budget of 4,000 positions examined, with four random opening plies. Draws count half a point to each side. The bars are the central 95% of 1,000 seeded parametric bootstrap refits, i.e. HOW FAR THIS TABLE WOULD MOVE IF THE SAME GAMES WERE PLAYED AGAIN. Its blind spot is stated rather than hidden (P36): `random` scored 0.0% against three of its five opponents, so it is resampled from p = 0, wins none in every resample, and gets a band far narrower than its real uncertainty. ## f4_parallel_speedup

- **Chapter:** 5. Metodologija
- **Files:** `f4_parallel_speedup.pdf`, `f4_parallel_speedup.png`
- **Sources:** `parallel_speedup.csv`, `parallel_control.csv`

Speedup against worker count on an Apple M3 with four performance and four efficiency cores, in a fanless chassis. The harness curve is one sweep of the tournament (180 games, 4,000-node budget, identical digest at every worker count). The control is a pure CPU-bound Python loop -- thirty-two identical perfectly balanced tasks, nothing crossing the process boundary but an integer -- a program that CANNOT be inefficient, so whatever it fails to achieve is the machine; it is measured five times and drawn as a median with its full range. **The honest reading is narrower than it first appears.** Above two workers this machine is reproducible only to about +-15%, so the harness is separated from the control at 6 and 8 workers and is indistinguishable from it at 2 and 4. What survives the noise is the claim that matters: at eight workers a program that cannot be wasteful does no better than the harness, so the missing speedup is the machine. os.cpu_count() is a count of schedulable contexts, not a speedup.

## What deliberately did not get a chart

- **minimax_vs_greedy** -- seven points; the argument is that the curve is NOT monotone (depth 3 below depth 2), which a table states more exactly than a line does
- **ordering/transposition/scout gain curves** -- gains of 0.8x-1.6x are flat lines on any axis; the numbers are the result, so they are tabulated
- **transposition_density** -- four numbers (the ceiling at depths 3/5/7/8)
- **mcts_vs_alphabeta** -- six rows whose point is that two budgets disagree by 26 points; a table puts the two budgets side by side, a chart would not
- **mcts_ablation** -- the shape IS that one switch sits at 6.9 sigma and every other inside 1.2 sigma -- which is read off a sigma column, not a plot
- **tournament pairing matrix** -- a 15-row round robin is a table; the Elo fit is the chart
- **deepening_under_a_clock** -- an overshoot distribution reported as median/p90/p99
